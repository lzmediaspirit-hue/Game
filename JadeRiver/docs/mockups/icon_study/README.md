equipment 104, HUD 73, techniques 66, status 34, markers 17) is redrawn. Everything here is original art, generated by
`tools/icons/study/icon_study.py` (deterministic: two builds give byte-identical PNGs). Nothing in `art/icons/`, the
manifest or game data was changed.

## Two things the drawing found before the styles

1. **A 64 px slot shows 52 px of icon.** `page.gd slot_box` draws the icon in `rect.grow(-6)` and the kit CSS insets
   `.k-slot > .ic` 6 px, so today's 64 px icons are already scaled to 52 with the page's nearest filter (review I2 in
   practice: pixels dropped unevenly). An HD 64-px icon would be too. The strip at the foot of sheet 01 shows the three
   ways out: keep 64 slots and box-filter to 52 (soft), grow the slot to 76 so the icon sits 1:1 (what sheets 02 and 04
   use; the tap target grows from 64 to 76), or keep 64 slots and draw the icon at inset 0 (the art keeps a 4 px margin,
   so the object overlaps the rim by 4). **The P4 style guide should decide this first**; the study recommends the 76
   px slot on pages.
2. **One description, any size.** Both styles are drawn from the same parametric description in a 64-px space
   (`study_icons.py`) through a painter interface (`study_lib.py`). The pixel painter re-renders the same description
   at 48 (`A native` on sheet 01), which is crisper than filtering the 64 down. So the HUD's 48 px technique slot and the
   32 px item slot can have native renders from the pipeline instead of a scaled texture, and the 780 icons need not be
   drawn twice.

## Style A, "HD pixel"

Native 64 x 64 art px for items, equipment and techniques (four times today's 32 x 32 detail), 32 for HUD glyphs, hard
pixel edges, shown 1:1. What changes against today's 32-px drawing:

- **Ramps of 7 steps** per material (`mat7`: the palette's 5 steps plus a deeper shadow and a specular), base at index 3,
  so a blade has a lit face, a ridge, a shade face and a rim in distinct tones.
- **Selective outline.** Inside the object the separation lines are the dark tone of the local material (the
  `Ramp.out` colour, 55% ink); the outer silhouette goes to near-ink only where the neighbouring body pixel is bright
  (78% ink beside a highlight, 42% beside a dark cloth), so a dark robe keeps a coloured edge and a pale blade a black one.
- **One light top-left, a rim light bottom-right**: a 1-px mist-blue line along the shadow edge of metal, glass, jade and
  porcelain (weaker on cloth and wood), which lifts the object off the dark slot.
- **Material textures as pixel patterns**, no dithering: grain lines along wood (every 3 px, with a knot rhythm), a
  bright sheen band and a dark reflection band across metal, through-lit jade and glass (light pooling in a crescent on
  the far side, a lighter core), fold lines on cloth, laid lines on paper, a wet sheen on clay.
- **Grade**: material sets change the blade, fittings, grip, wrap, gem and blade work (spirit: a lightning zigzag; sage:
  ember-glass beads; will: star dots and a starlight edge); Mystic and above add stepped glow bands (120 / 64 / 28
  alpha). The slot's grade rim is the UI's.
- **HUD glyphs** keep today's language (pale gold face, ink outline) one step richer: a lit edge, a warm shade edge,
  one highlight, a ridge on the blade.
- **Empty slot**: a 64-px cloud seal (two curls on a base line) in jade shadow at 35%.

At 1x it is crisp in the HD frames and reads at 48 by re-rendering; the pixel look stays the same language as the world
sprites, the status icons and the numbers. At 52 (box-filtered) it goes soft, like today.

## Style B, "painted icon"

The same descriptions rendered at 256 px with smooth shading and box-downsampled to 64 (HUD glyphs 128 -> 32, and a
native 64 for the large ring), so edges are anti-aliased but still crisp at 1x. What it changes:

- **Smooth normals** (a quarter-round pillow inside each part's edge, true sphere normals for round parts, two tilted
  faces for a blade) lit from the top-left, a **specular** per material kind (metal and gems shiny, cloth and paper
  matte), **ambient occlusion** where a part overlaps another, a soft dark contour on the silhouette, a soft cast
  shadow.
- **Textures as gradients**: a wave along wood grain, a gaussian sheen on metal, through-lit glass and jade with a soft
  far-side light, folds on cloth.
- **Grade**: the same material sets, plus a **soft inner glow** (a light just inside the edge) and a soft outer glow in
  the grade colour for Mystic and above.
- The driftglass blends violet into teal with soft bands, the technique emblems become domed discs with a keyline under
  the mark, the HUD glyphs are bevelled gold with a soft shadow so they still read on the jade disc.

At 1x it reads as a hand-painted mobile RPG icon; it scales to 48 and 32 by filtering with no loss of character (sheet
03), and the grade glow is the gentler of the two. It is softer than the pixel HUD glyphs, the status icons and the world
sprites it sits beside, and the bag page (04_bag_B) reads as a different game from the fight screen.

## Cost of converting the whole set

Both styles need the same first step: **every icon redrawn in a 64-px description**. Today's drawing code is in 32-px
integer space with hand-placed pixels (rects, Bresenham lines, ASCII stamps, single-pixel highlights); rendering it at
2x gives 2-px lines and blocks, not detail. The family templates carry most of the work (pills: 4 vessels + 33 marks;
weapons: 9 builders x 7 grades; techniques: one emblem + 66 marks; HUD: 73 ASCII blocks), so the redraw is per template
plus a smaller pass per icon for its own detail.

**Style A pipeline change** (the study's `study_lib.py` is the prototype; about a day to fold into `tools/icons/`):

- `pix.py`: a **64-px canvas mode**, `Canvas(64)` with the scaled coordinate builders (`SCanvas`: masks take icon-space
  coordinates and a scale), 7-level shading bands, the `field` mode for cylinders and blades, the `Frame` helper for
  diagonal objects, and three finishing passes on the canvas: texture (per material kind), rim light, the selective
  outline. The existing 32-px API stays, so untouched families keep building.
- `palette.py`: `Mat` (7 colours + outline + kind) beside `Ramp`, `mat7()` for every ramp, material kinds
  (metal, gold, glass, jade, wood, cloth, silk, clay, paper, porcelain, gem, leather, ink), and grade kits for
  sovereign, will and sphere (missing today).
- `registry.py` / `build_icons.py`: `FAMILY_SIZE` items, equipment, techniques = 64 at scale 1, hud = 32 at scale 1;
  size variants per family (techniques also at 48 for the HUD ring, items also at 32 for the 52 ring) written as
  `<id>@48.png` and listed in the manifest; contact sheets in 76-px slots at 1x and 2x.
- The UI: `page.gd` slot size 76 (or the inset), `hud.gd` reading the 48 and 32 variants, `kit.css` to match. Status and
  markers can stay at 12 -> 24 for now or move to a native 24 later; they are a separate, small pass.

**Style B pipeline change**: everything above (the descriptions are shared), plus a second renderer (`paint.py`: the
supersampled canvas, distance fields, normals, specular, occlusion, contour, glows, premultiplied downsample; about 500
lines, prototyped in `PaintedPainter`), soft-friendly decals (a hard 1-px decal at 64 becomes a 4-px stroke at 256, and
several icons already needed a `style == 'B'` branch), material tuning per kind, and a full build of about 4 minutes
instead of 15 seconds (0.15 to 0.35 s per icon at 256). The HUD glyphs, status icons and markers would stay pixel (they
are drawn over pixel sprites and at 24 px), so the game would carry two icon languages.

## Recommendation: Style A, HD pixel

- It keeps one visual language: the world is pixel sprites drawn with nearest-neighbour rendering (`AGENTS.md` rule 3),
  the HUD glyphs, status icons, markers and damage numbers are pixel, and the approved P3 mockups resolved conflicts C6
  and C7 as "HD frames, pixel icons and sprites". Style B puts painted objects on a pixel HUD next to pixel drops.
- It is the smaller change: the pipeline stays a pixel pipeline with a canvas mode, seven-step ramps and three finishing
  passes; a full build stays seconds, so the review loop stays fast.
- It is crisp at 1x by construction, and the parametric description gives native 48 and 32 renders, so the non-integer
  scaling that made today's icons soft (I2) goes away instead of being hidden by a filter.
- The soft grade glow that Style B does better belongs to the UI anyway (`page.gd _pill_glow` already draws a soft halo
  behind pill icons by quality; the slot can draw one by grade behind any icon), so A can have it without baking it in.

What to take from B into A: the domed technique disc with the keyline under the mark, the softer transitions in glass
(more steps in the far-side crescent), and the UI halo for grade.

## Conversion plan

1. **Decide the display sizes** (P4 style guide): the 76 px slot with the icon at 1:1 on pages; 48 techniques and 32
   items on the HUD from native variants. Land `page.gd`, `hud.gd`, `kit.css` and the kit sheet first.
2. **Pipeline** (one agent, one day): the 64-px canvas mode, `Mat` ramps, the finishing passes, size variants, contact
   sheets in slots, the missing grade kits. Its test: the twelve study icons rebuilt through `build_icons.py` match the
   study within a few pixels, and every untouched family still builds byte-identical.
3. **Families**, in the order of the table above (visibility first: HUD, techniques, pills, weapons, armour; then the
   big item groups). After step 2, **four or five agents in parallel**, one per family module (`families/*.py` are
   independent; only the manifest is shared, and a full build regenerates it): hud | techniques | pills + weapons +
   armour | herbs + minerals + beast_parts | the rest in two halves. A family is converted whole; a half-converted family
   would mix 32 and 64 icons on one page.
4. **Review loop per family**: (a) `build_icons.py --only <family> --review-dir` and look at the contact sheets at 1x in
   the 76 slot and at 2x; (b) an in-context composite through the mockup renderer (the family's page: the bag grid, the
   technique bar, the HUD rings) at 1280 x 720; (c) the checklist: one bold object, the silhouette reads at 48, light
   top-left with the rim on the shadow edge, the outline rule, no stray pixels, palette tokens only, no clipping at the
   canvas edge, the grade material rule (form and trim change, never colour alone), consistent weight with the
   neighbouring family; (d) the user approves the family sheet, then the script, the PNGs and the manifest are committed
   together; (e) the next family starts on the same page only after the first is approved, so the page never mixes.
5. **Status and markers** last, only if the native 24 is wanted; they are already integer-scaled.
